## [Shutterstock Fined $35M for Hard-to-Cancel Subscriptions](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/shutterstock-pay-35-million-settle-ftc-allegations-over-illegal-subscription-cancellation-practices) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The FTC fined Shutterstock $35 million for allegedly making it difficult for users to cancel their subscriptions, violating the FTC's 1973 Negative Option Rule. This case highlights the ongoing issue of deceptive subscription practices and could push for standardized cancellation flows across the US, impacting both consumers and businesses. The settlement requires Shutterstock to make cancellation as easy as signing up, and it must obtain explicit consent before charging consumers.

hackernews · Lihh27 · May 18, 19:50 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48184635)

**Background**: The FTC's Negative Option Rule requires companies to clearly disclose subscription terms and provide a simple cancellation method. Shutterstock allegedly required customers to call to cancel even though they could sign up online, and continued charging after cancellation requests. This enforcement aims to prevent such 'dark patterns.'

**Discussion**: Commenters largely support the FTC action, with many calling for a nationwide law requiring cancellation methods to match sign-up methods. Some shared negative experiences with Shutterstock, and others suggested a unified subscription management system.

GDS, which oversees UK government digital services, disagrees and has publicly advised keeping repos open.

## [US CS Graduate Unemployment Hits 7%; Top Universities See Enrollment Crash](https://wallstreetcn.com/member/articles/3772222) ⭐️ 8.0/10

New York Fed data for early 2026 shows that US computer science graduates aged 22-27 face a 7% unemployment rate, while top universities like Princeton and UC Berkeley report drastic enrollment drops of 50% and 59% respectively in their CS programs. This signals a significant correction in the tech job market, impacting new graduates and potentially reshaping university enrollment priorities, especially as high-paying sectors like banking and consulting also cut hiring amid a non-recession economy. Princeton's CS majors fell from 150 to 74 in two years; UC Berkeley's EECS graduates are projected to drop from 1,029 to about 350 (59% decline). JPMorgan's internship acceptance rate dropped from 2.8% to 0.7%, and PwC plans to cut entry-level hiring from 3,242 to 2,197 over three years.
